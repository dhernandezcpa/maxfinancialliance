Career paths

Why EA? Why CPA?

Both credentials can lead to a practice of your own. They differ in who grants them, what they let you do, and what it takes to earn them. The Enrolled Agent credential is federal and granted by the IRS. The CPA license is granted by each state board of accountancy, following the model law that NASBA and the AICPA publish, the Uniform Accountancy Act.

Why EA?

The fastest route to full IRS representation

Enrolled agents are federally authorized tax practitioners with unlimited rights to represent any taxpayer before the IRS, in any tax matter, in every state.

What it takes

  • A current PTIN, then the three-part Special Enrollment Examination: Individuals; Businesses; and Representation, Practices and Procedures
  • No college degree or prior experience is required
  • Pass all three parts within three years of passing the first
  • Apply for enrollment on Form 23 within one year of passing the third part; the IRS reviews your own tax compliance and background
  • Keep it current with 72 hours of continuing education every three years, at least 16 each year, including 2 of ethics

Best for

Tax-focused professionals, bookkeepers adding tax and representation, and candidates who want a credential within months while they work.

Keep in mind

Enrolled agents may not perform audits or reviews or use the CPA title.

Why CPA?

The widest scope in the profession

CPAs hold a state license. Licensed CPA firms are the only ones permitted to perform audits and reviews, and CPAs also have unlimited IRS representation rights and can offer tax, advisory and CFO services.

What it takes: the three E's

  • Education with an accounting concentration, under one of the pathways below
  • Examination: three core sections (AUD, FAR, REG) and one discipline of your choice (BAR, ISC or TCP)
  • Experience verified by a licensed CPA, one or two years depending on the pathway
  • Each state sets the window for passing all sections. In Florida, credit for a section released on or after January 1, 2024 lasts 30 months.

Best for

Professionals who want to perform attest work, serve larger and regulated clients, or own a CPA firm.

Keep in mind

Licensure is state by state. Check your state board's current rules before you plan your coursework.

Continuity

A plan if your accountant steps back

When a member plans to retire or sell, the Alliance has a process to match the practice with a qualified successor, so your records and your relationship are handed over with care rather than left to chance.

What stays the same for you

Membership adds support behind your accountant. It does not change who you work with.

  • You keep working with your own accountant, under your own engagement letter with their firm.
  • You choose your professional. Being referred for one service never moves you to another firm.
  • If another professional takes part in your matter, you are told in writing first, including any payment between the firms. You pay nothing extra for that arrangement.
  • Audits, reviews and compilations are performed only by licensed CPA firms.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a CPA license to join?

No. You don't have to be a CPA to bring your practice to top CPA-level standards of excellence. Bookkeepers, accountants, enrolled agents, CPAs and credential candidates all join. Each member practices within its own credential, and work that needs a credential you do not hold goes to an eligible professional.

I keep failing a part of the EA or CPA exam. Can you help?

Yes. Max Financial Academy offers study materials, practice questions for the Enrolled Agent exam, a study plan built around your work schedule, and progress coaching. Schedule a 30-minute meeting and bring your score reports.

Who owns my clients?

You do. A referral never transfers a client. Work referred for a specialist service returns to you, and every client remains free to choose its professional.

How is my level set?

From the gross receipts on your practice's most recent filed return. If you have not yet filed one, you start at Launch. Levels are reviewed each January; a practice that has passed its level's revenue band moves up for the new year, and no member moves down during its first 12 months. You may also choose a higher level. Practices above $1 million join at Million Plus.

What happens to consulting hours I don't use?

Hours pool by calendar quarter. Hours left at the end of a quarter expire. Time beyond your allowance is billed at your level's member consulting rate, and only after you approve an estimate.

How long is the commitment, and how are fees paid?

The initial term is 12 months, then membership continues month to month. The initial consulting fee is paid in three installments: at signing, at the draft diagnostic and at the final. The monthly fee is paid by ACH on the first business day of each month. We review the fees for your level in our first meeting.

I plan to retire in a few years. Why join now?

Because the best successions are prepared, not negotiated at the last minute. Joining early gives you time to make the practice less dependent on you, and puts you in front of members who could become your partner or your buyer. You approve every introduction, nothing is required of you, and you remain free to sell to anyone.

How is the Alliance different from selling to private equity?

A private equity buyer pays for ownership of your firm and shares in its control and its future value. The Alliance asks for no ownership. You pay a membership fee for the support you use, and the firm, its decisions and the value you build stay yours. If you later decide to sell, you choose the buyer.

Will my firm lose its name or independence?

No. You keep your firm name, brand, prices and clients. You may say you are a "Member, Max Financial Alliance," but you do not use the Max Financial name as your own, and members are not partners, employees or offices of Brickell CPA, Inc.
